Abstract

Continuous video scales in cost with camera count, resolution and recording duration, yet many monitoring tasks seek events rather than complete images and can operate on a compact single-pixel measurement stream. Recovering a chosen interval as video is then desirable, but at 0.24% sampling the scene correlation image looks like noise and per-clip optimization takes minutes. Correlating the residual of an estimate with the coding patterns nevertheless gives a residual image, the gradient of a centered measurement loss. Lifted through a pretrained video decoder, its agreement with the true error exceeds that of control decoders. Video-latent inversion (VLI) then outperforms frame-wise image-latent reconstruction at 1,000 bucket values per frame. Measurement-Gradient-Conditioned Video Flow (MGV-Flow), trained once to read the lifted residual image with preceding frames, reconstructs five frames in 1.55 s at a median peak signal-to-noise ratio of 22.9 dB, against 398 s and 18.1 dB for VLI, with the largest gains in high-motion scenes. Directional fidelity ranks 30 coding configurations and selects one needing a third fewer bucket values. On a physical system, MGV-Flow outperforms per-frame reconstruction without retraining. A sliding window over the bucket-value stream yields 3.2 frames per second on one RTX 4090 and, in theory, 20 frames per second with a 0.23 s delay on one data-centre accelerator. The correlation that founded single-pixel imaging thus becomes feedback a generative model learns to read.

Results

1

Lifting residual images into a video latent space makes inversion scalable

Through the pretrained video decoder, the noise-like residual image becomes a structured correction that agrees with the true error better than through re-initialized or weight-shuffled decoders. Video-latent inversion (VLI), which reconstructs five frames jointly in this latent space, outperforms pixel networks and frame-wise image-latent reconstruction (GILM) when bucket values are scarce.

At 1,000 bucket values per frame, averaged over four resolutions and five test videos, VLI reaches 18.02 dB against 16.44 dB for GILM and 11.59 dB for a U-Net. At 856×480 with 10,000 bucket values per frame, joint inversion needs 56.3% less computation and 72.8% less peak GPU memory than GILM.

2

Reading instead of consuming residual images enables amortized video reconstruction

MGV-Flow is trained once to read the lifted residual image together with the history frames. A five-frame clip takes 1.55 s at K = 5 instead of 397.99 s with VLI, a median speedup of 256×, while median PSNR rises from 18.12 to 22.92 dB. VLI fits the measurements far more closely, yet in none of 21 cases does it reach the PSNR of MGV-Flow, because at 0.24% sampling many videos fit the measurements almost equally well.

Setting the measurement inputs to zero lowers PSNR by 1.57 dB and inputs from another video lower it by 0.68 dB, so the model reads the measured scene. The benefit of reading grows with motion (Spearman 0.738), where the history predicts the target least.

3

Directional fidelity of residual images reveals coding-pattern utility

Directional fidelity measures whether the lifted residual image points toward the true latent correction, at the cost of one residual image and one decoder vector-Jacobian product and without running a reconstruction. Across 30 configurations of pattern family, grain size and measurement count, it predicts reconstruction quality with a Spearman correlation of 0.779 (95% confidence interval 0.644 to 0.862).

Ranked on calibration windows, the top configuration, a Walsh basis at 16 px, matches the median-ranked configuration at 3,000 bucket values per frame with only 2,000 and exceeds it in all eight held-out windows. The best pattern also depends on the video autoencoder.

4

On physical measurements, reading transfers without retraining

On bucket values acquired by a physical single-pixel system with structured illumination, MGV-Flow, neither retrained nor fine-tuned, predicts frames 6 to 10 of two animated film excerpts from 10,000 measured bucket values per frame and five ground-truth history frames. It reaches an equalized PSNR of 10.25 dB against 8.63 dB for GILM, the best per-frame method, and produces five frames in 7.85 s while GILM needs 44.13 s for each frame.

Bucket values of the other clip lower PSNR in all six runs, and the measurement residual falls to 0.54 to 0.61 of its initial value against 0.65 to 0.69 for the other clip, so the model reads the physical measurements.
